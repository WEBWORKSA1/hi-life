The savings-by-age milestones

A well-known guideline from a major US retirement provider suggests aiming for roughly 1× your salary saved by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67. It assumes you save about 15% of income each year from age 25 (including any employer match), keep more than half in stocks over your lifetime, and retire at 67 with a similar lifestyle.

Adjusting for your plans

Retiring earlier means fewer years to save and more years to fund, so the target rises; retiring later lowers it. Planning a simpler lifestyle lowers the target, and a more generous one raises it. Use the selectors to see your adjusted milestone.

If you are behind

  1. Capture the full employer match — it is an instant return.
  2. Raise your savings rate by 1% every year, ideally timed with raises.
  3. Use catch-up contributions after 50 where your country allows them.
  4. Consider working a couple of years longer or part-time in early retirement.

Milestones are aspirational signposts, not grades. The trend matters more than any single year.

Frequently asked questions

Should I count my home?

No. The milestones refer to retirement savings and investments, not home equity.

Does my partner’s savings count?

Check each person against their own salary, or combine both salaries and savings for a household view.

What if I started saving late?

Focus on the monthly amount shown: steady increases and catch-up contributions can close large gaps over 10–15 years.
