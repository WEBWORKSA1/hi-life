What is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure is the number of calories your body uses in a day: your basal metabolic rate (BMR, the energy to keep you alive at rest) multiplied by an activity factor. Eating around your TDEE tends to keep weight stable. This calculator uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, one of the more accurate formulas for most adults.

Using the numbers sensibly

  • Treat the result as a starting estimate. Track your weight trend for 2–3 weeks and adjust by 100–200 calories if needed.
  • Slow change is easier to keep. Small deficits protect muscle, mood and sleep.
  • Protein, fibre, sleep and strength training make any calorie target easier to stick to.

About BMI

BMI (weight ÷ height²) is a quick population screening tool. It does not separate muscle from fat, and healthy ranges can differ by age and ethnicity. Waist size, blood tests and how you feel are just as important. For personal advice, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a calorie calculator?

Formulas are usually within about 10% for most people. Your own weight trend is the best guide.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

The activity level already includes typical exercise, so you usually should not add them again.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

No. Muscular people can have a high BMI and low body fat.
