Why look at your life in weeks?

A long life is roughly four thousand weeks. Laid out as a grid, that number feels both short and full of possibility. The idea was popularised by writer Tim Urban's "Your Life in Weeks" essay and echoes the ancient Stoic practice of remembering that time is limited — not to be gloomy, but to spend it on purpose.

How to use your calendar

  • Pick a theme for the next 52 boxes. One year, one main goal.
  • Protect weekly rituals. A weekly call with a parent or friend adds up to hundreds of conversations.
  • Plan for the long game. Health and money habits started now are paid back many times over in the later boxes.

About life expectancy

We default to 85 years as a planning age. Many people live longer — financial planners often recommend planning to age 90 or beyond so you do not outlive your savings. Your birth date is used only in your browser and never sent anywhere.
