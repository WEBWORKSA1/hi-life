How the drawdown works

Each month your remaining balance earns your expected return, then your withdrawal is taken out. Pension or government benefits reduce what you need to withdraw. Once a year the withdrawal rises with inflation so your buying power stays steady. When the balance hits zero, the money has run out.

Three numbers worth knowing

  • Years it lasts at the withdrawal you want.
  • The 30-year withdrawal: the most you can take, rising with inflation, so the money lasts exactly 30 years.
  • The principal-preserving withdrawal: roughly what you can take in today’s money if you only spend real growth.

Sequence risk

A market fall early in retirement does more damage than the same fall later, because you are selling investments while they are down. A cash buffer of one to two years of spending and flexible withdrawals in bad years are the most common defences.

Frequently asked questions

What withdrawal rate is safe?

Around 4% of the starting balance, rising with inflation, has historically lasted 30 years in most US markets. Longer retirements usually need less.

Should I include my home?

Only if you plan to sell or downsize. Otherwise count only money you can actually withdraw.

Are taxes included?

No. If withdrawals are taxable, plan for the after-tax amount you need.
