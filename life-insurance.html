The DIME method

DIME is a quick, widely used way to estimate a life insurance need:

  • Debt — all debts other than the mortgage, plus final expenses.
  • Income — your yearly income times the number of years your family would rely on it.
  • Mortgage — the balance needed to pay off the home.
  • Education — expected costs for your children's schooling.

Subtract existing cover and savings your family could use, and the remainder is your coverage gap.

Term or permanent?

Term life covers a set period (often 10–30 years) and is usually far cheaper per dollar of cover. It suits temporary needs: raising children, paying off a mortgage, replacing income during working years. Permanent life (whole or universal) lasts for life and builds cash value but costs much more; it can fit estate planning or lifelong dependants.

Before you buy

  • Compare quotes from several insurers or an independent broker — prices for the same cover vary.
  • Work-provided cover often ends when you leave the job.
  • Review your cover after major life events: marriage, a new child, a new home or a big raise.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need life insurance if I am single?

Usually only if someone depends on you financially or would be left with your debts (for example co-signed loans).

Should stay-at-home parents have cover?

Yes. Replacing childcare and household work costs real money; many families insure that value.

How long should my term be?

Long enough to cover the period your family depends on your income — commonly until the youngest child is independent or the mortgage is paid.
