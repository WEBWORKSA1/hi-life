The 28/36 rule

Many lenders use two ratios. Your housing costs (mortgage payment, property tax, insurance and fees) should stay under about 28% of gross monthly income, and all debt payments together under about 36%. The calculator finds the highest price that satisfies both, then shows a more comfortable price at 25/33.

What the bank approves vs what you can live with

Lenders may approve more than you should spend. Your real limit depends on childcare, retirement saving, commuting and how much buffer you want. Use the comfortable figure if you want room to save and enjoy life.

Hidden costs to budget for

  • Closing costs, often around 2–5% of the price.
  • Maintenance: plan for roughly 1% of the home's value per year.
  • Mortgage insurance if your down payment is under 20%.
  • Moving, furniture and utility set-up.

Rules vary by country, so check local lending limits and taxes. Mortgage rates change daily — compare at least three lenders or a broker.

Frequently asked questions

Is 28/36 based on gross or take-home pay?

Gross (before-tax) income, which is how most lenders measure it.

How much should I put down?

20% avoids mortgage insurance in many markets, but smaller down payments are common. Keep an emergency fund after closing.

Should I choose a 15- or 30-year mortgage?

A 15-year loan costs much less interest but has higher payments. A 30-year loan is more flexible; you can still prepay.
