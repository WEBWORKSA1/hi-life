What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 budget splits your after-tax income into three buckets: about 50% for needs (the bills you must pay), 30% for wants (the things that make life enjoyable), and 20% for your future (savings, investing and paying off debt faster than the minimum). It is popular because it is simple enough to keep using.

How to sort your spending

  • Needs are costs you would still pay if you lost your job: rent or mortgage, utilities, basic groceries, insurance, transport to work and minimum loan payments.
  • Wants are optional: restaurants, streaming, upgrades, holidays and most shopping.
  • Savings includes emergency fund deposits, retirement contributions and any debt payment above the minimum.

When to adjust the split

In high-cost cities, needs often take 60% of income. That is fine for a while, but protect the 20% by trimming wants first. If you have high-interest debt, temporarily move part of "wants" into "savings & debt" until the debt is gone. Once you are comfortable, push savings toward 25–30%; it is the fastest route to financial independence.

Frequently asked questions

Is 50/30/20 based on gross or net income?

Net (take-home) income — what actually lands in your bank account after tax and payroll deductions.

Do retirement contributions taken from my paycheck count?

Yes. Add pre-tax retirement contributions to both your income and your savings bucket to see your true savings rate.

What if my needs are over 50%?

Cut wants first, then look at the big three needs: housing, transport and insurance. Small fixes there beat dozens of tiny cuts.
