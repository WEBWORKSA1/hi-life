How sleep cycles work

Through the night your brain moves through cycles of light sleep, deep sleep and REM (dreaming) sleep. Each cycle lasts roughly 90 minutes, though it varies from person to person and through the night. Waking at the end of a cycle often feels easier than being pulled out of deep sleep.

How much sleep do adults need?

Most health bodies recommend 7–9 hours for adults. That is usually five or six full cycles. Consistently sleeping less is linked to worse mood, focus, appetite control and long-term health.

Seven habits for better sleep

  1. Keep the same wake-up time every day, including weekends.
  2. Get daylight in your eyes within an hour of waking.
  3. Stop caffeine about 8 hours before bed.
  4. Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet.
  5. Dim screens and lights an hour before bed.
  6. Avoid heavy meals and alcohol late in the evening.
  7. If you cannot sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy.

If you snore loudly, stop breathing at night or feel exhausted despite enough sleep, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sleep 6 or 7.5 hours?

7.5 hours (five cycles) is closer to the 7–9 hours most adults need. Waking between cycles helps but does not replace enough sleep.

Are sleep cycles exactly 90 minutes?

No — they range from about 70 to 120 minutes. 90 minutes is a useful average for planning.

Do naps count?

A 10–20 minute nap can boost alertness without grogginess. Longer naps may affect night sleep.
