Why net worth beats income

Income tells you how much flows in; net worth tells you how much you keep. Two households earning the same salary can be decades apart in financial freedom. Tracking net worth monthly turns vague goals into a single number that should rise over time.

How to value your assets

  • Use today's market value for investments and retirement accounts.
  • For a home, use a conservative estimate (recent comparable sales minus about 6–8% for selling costs).
  • Cars lose value quickly; use a private-sale estimate, not what you paid.
  • Leave out personal items like furniture and electronics unless they are valuable and easy to sell.

The "wealth benchmark"

The classic rule from The Millionaire Next Door says your expected net worth is your age multiplied by your yearly pre-tax household income, divided by ten. It is a rough yardstick, not a judgement — younger people and anyone with student loans will often be below it. What matters is the trend: up and to the right.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include my home?

Yes, but also look at your net worth without it. Your home is where you live, so it is not money you can easily spend.

Is a negative net worth bad?

It is common early in a career, especially with student loans. Focus on making the number rise each month.

How often should I track it?

Monthly or quarterly is plenty. Daily checking just adds noise from market swings.
