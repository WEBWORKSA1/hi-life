Avalanche or snowball?

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and throw every spare dollar at one target. When that debt is gone, its payment "rolls" onto the next one, so payments snowball over time.

  • Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first. It is mathematically cheapest and usually fastest.
  • Snowball targets the smallest balance first. You clear accounts sooner, and that early win keeps many people motivated.

The calculator shows the true cost difference for your debts. Often it is small, so pick the method you will actually stick with.

Ways to speed it up

  1. Find the extra. Even a small fixed extra payment can cut years off.
  2. Lower the rate. Ask card issuers for a reduction, or consider a 0% balance transfer or a lower-rate consolidation loan — but only if you stop adding new debt.
  3. Use windfalls. Tax refunds, bonuses and side income go straight to the target debt.
  4. Protect the plan. Keep a one-month starter emergency fund so a surprise bill does not land back on a card.

Frequently asked questions

Should I invest or pay off debt first?

Pay off high-interest debt (roughly above 7–8%) first. Still take any employer retirement match — it is an instant return.

Is debt consolidation a good idea?

It can be if the new rate is lower, fees are small and you close or stop using the old cards. Otherwise it just moves the problem.

Does paying off debt raise my credit score?

Usually yes, especially lowering credit card balances relative to their limits.
