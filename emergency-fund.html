How big should an emergency fund be?

The common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. This calculator adjusts that range for your risk: variable income, dependants and being the only earner all push the target up, to a maximum of twelve months.

Start small, then build

  1. Starter fund: one month of essentials, or a fixed amount you can reach quickly, to stop small surprises from becoming debt.
  2. Pay down high-interest debt while keeping the starter fund.
  3. Full fund: build to your target before investing heavily outside retirement accounts.

What counts as an emergency?

Job loss, urgent medical or dental costs, essential car or home repairs, and emergency travel. Not holidays, sales or planned purchases — give those their own savings goals so the safety net stays intact.

Frequently asked questions

Should my emergency fund be invested?

No. Keep it in cash you can reach within a day or two — a high-yield savings account is ideal. The goal is safety, not growth.

Can I use a credit card instead?

A card is a backup, not a fund. Borrowing at high interest during a crisis makes recovery harder.

Should couples have one fund or two?

Either works. What matters is that the total covers the household's essential costs for the target number of months.
