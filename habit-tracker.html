Why tiny habits win

Big goals fail when they rely on motivation. Small, specific habits tied to something you already do ("after I pour my coffee, I write three priorities") rely on routine instead. Once the habit is automatic, you can make it bigger.

Four rules for habits that stick

  1. Make it obvious: decide the time and place.
  2. Make it easy: shrink it until it takes under two minutes to start.
  3. Make it satisfying: tick the box here — the streak is the reward.
  4. Never miss twice: one missed day is life; two is the start of a new habit.

Habits that lift every area of life

  • Money: check your balance and log spending for two minutes a day.
  • Health: a 20-minute walk, a glass of water before each meal.
  • Career: 30 minutes of skill practice.
  • Relationships: one message to someone you care about.
  • Mind: five minutes of journaling or breathing before bed.

Frequently asked questions

Where are my habits stored?

Only in this browser. Clearing site data or switching devices starts fresh.

How long does a habit take to form?

Research suggests anywhere from a few weeks to several months, depending on the habit and the person. Consistency matters more than the count.

What if I break my streak?

Start again the next day. Long-term consistency beats a perfect streak.
