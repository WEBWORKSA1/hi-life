Stress is not the enemy. Short bursts help you focus and rise to challenges. The problem is stress that never switches off. Here is how to reset in minutes and build a calmer baseline.

## In the moment: four fast resets

**1. Physiological sigh (1 minute).** Take a deep breath in through the nose, then a second short "top-up" inhale, then a long slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat two or three times. Long exhales help activate the body's calming response.

**2. Box breathing (2–4 minutes).** Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. Used by athletes and first responders to steady nerves.

**3. 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (2 minutes).** Name five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear, two you can smell and one you can taste. It pulls attention back to the present.

**4. Move (5–10 minutes).** A brisk walk, stairs or a few push-ups burns off stress hormones and lifts mood.

## The daily 10-minute reset

1. **2 minutes** — breathing (either technique above)
2. **3 minutes** — brain dump: write every worry and task on paper
3. **3 minutes** — circle the one thing you can act on today and schedule it
4. **2 minutes** — write three things that went well or you are grateful for

Track it in the [Habit Tracker](habit-tracker.html) for two weeks and notice the difference.

## Build long-term resilience

- **Sleep**: stress and poor sleep feed each other. Fix sleep first. See our [sleep guide](guides/sleep-better.html).
- **Exercise**: regular activity is one of the most reliable mood boosters.
- **Connection**: talking to someone you trust lowers the load. Isolation magnifies it.
- **Boundaries**: decide when work ends; turn off notifications after that time.
- **Money buffer**: financial stress is one of the most common sources of worry. An [emergency fund](guides/emergency-fund.html) buys peace of mind.
- **Purpose**: knowing what matters to you makes hard days easier to carry.

## When stress becomes something more

If you feel overwhelmed most days for more than two weeks, lose interest in things you used to enjoy, have trouble functioning at work or home, or have thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out to a doctor, a licensed mental health professional or a local crisis line right away. You do not have to handle it alone.
