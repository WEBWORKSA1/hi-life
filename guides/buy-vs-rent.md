"Renting is throwing money away" is one of the most repeated money myths. Owning can build wealth, but so can renting and investing the difference. The right answer depends on prices, rates, how long you will stay and how you live.

## The unrecoverable costs

A fair comparison looks at money you never get back:

**Owning**: mortgage interest, property tax, maintenance, insurance, and the opportunity cost of your down payment (what it could have earned invested).
**Renting**: rent and renter's insurance.

Principal repayments on a mortgage are not "spent" — they become equity — so they do not count as a cost.

## The 5% rule

A quick shortcut popularised by some financial educators: the yearly unrecoverable cost of owning is roughly **5% of the home's value** (about 1% property tax + 1% maintenance + ~3% cost of capital). Divide the home price by 20 to get a monthly-ish yearly figure, then divide by 12.

*Example*: a 500,000 home × 5% = 25,000 a year ≈ 2,083 a month. If you can rent a similar home for less than that, renting and investing the difference may leave you wealthier. Rates, taxes and local prices change the numbers, so treat it as a starting point.

## How long will you stay?

Buying and selling are expensive: closing costs, moving, and selling fees can add up to several percent of the price each way. Most analyses suggest you need to stay **around five years or more** to come out ahead of renting. Expect a job change, relationship change or move abroad? Renting keeps options open.

## Benefits that are not in the spreadsheet

**Owning**: stability, freedom to renovate, forced saving through principal payments, protection from rent increases.
**Renting**: flexibility, no surprise repair bills, less time spent on maintenance, easier to move for opportunity.

## A pre-purchase checklist

1. Emergency fund still intact **after** the down payment and closing costs.
2. Total housing costs within roughly 28% of gross income — check with the [Home Affordability Calculator](home-affordability.html).
3. No high-interest debt.
4. Stable income and plans to stay 5+ years.
5. Pre-approval from at least three lenders or a broker.
6. A professional inspection before you commit.

## If you rent, invest the difference

Renting only wins financially if you actually invest the money you would have spent on the extra costs of owning. Automate it.

## Bottom line

Run the numbers for your city. Buy when it fits your life and your budget — not because of pressure or fear of missing out.
