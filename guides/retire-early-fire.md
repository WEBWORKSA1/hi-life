FIRE stands for **Financial Independence, Retire Early**. The goal is to build enough invested wealth that work becomes optional. Some people stop working entirely; many switch to part-time, passion projects or simply negotiate from a position of strength. Here is how it works without the hype.

## Your FIRE number

Your FIRE number is the amount of invested money that can support your yearly spending indefinitely (or for a very long time). The common shortcut:

> FIRE number = yearly spending × 25

That comes from the "4% rule": historically, withdrawing about 4% of a diversified portfolio in the first year and adjusting for inflation lasted 30 years in most market conditions. Early retirees with 40–50 year horizons often use 3.25–3.5% instead, which means 29–31 times spending. Plug your numbers into the [Retirement & FIRE Calculator](retirement.html).

## Why savings rate matters more than income

Your savings rate does two jobs at once: it grows your investments **and** shows you can live on less, which lowers the target. Rough illustration, assuming moderate real returns and starting from zero:

| Savings rate | Approx. years to financial independence |
|---|---|
| 10% | 50+ |
| 25% | ~32 |
| 50% | ~17 |
| 65% | ~10 |

The exact numbers depend on returns, but the pattern is consistent: each jump in savings rate cuts years off the timeline.

## Flavours of FIRE

- **Lean FIRE**: a lower target for a frugal lifestyle.
- **Fat FIRE**: a higher target that supports more spending and travel.
- **Barista FIRE**: enough to cover most costs, with part-time work covering the rest (and sometimes health benefits).
- **Coast FIRE**: you have saved enough that, with no further contributions, growth alone should reach your full target by traditional retirement age. You only need to cover current living costs.

## How people get there

1. **Increase the gap** between income and spending: raise income (skills, job changes, side businesses) and cut big fixed costs.
2. **Invest the gap** in low-cost, diversified index funds, using tax-advantaged accounts first.
3. **Avoid lifestyle creep**: when income rises, save most of the raise.
4. **Stay the course** through market drops; selling in a panic is the costliest mistake.

## Risks to plan for

- **Sequence risk**: a big market fall right after you stop working hurts more than one later. Flexible spending and a cash buffer help.
- **Healthcare costs**, especially before government programs begin.
- **Inflation and longer life**: plan for a long retirement.
- **Purpose**: many early retirees say the hardest part is not money but meaning. Plan what you are retiring *to*.

## Is FIRE for you?

You do not need to retire at 40 for FIRE thinking to help. Even partial financial independence — enough to take a year off, change careers or say no to a bad boss — is life-changing. Start by knowing your number and your savings rate.
