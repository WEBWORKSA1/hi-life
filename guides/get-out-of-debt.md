Debt is not a moral failure; it is a maths problem with an emotional side. Solve both and you will get out faster than you think.

## Step 1: Stop adding new debt

Take the cards out of your wallet and remove them from shopping sites. Switch to debit or cash for daily spending while you pay off balances. You cannot empty a bath with the tap running.

## Step 2: Build a small buffer

Before attacking debt aggressively, save a starter emergency fund — about one month of essentials, or a smaller fixed amount if needed. This stops the next surprise bill from going straight back on a card. See our [emergency fund guide](guides/emergency-fund.html).

## Step 3: List everything

Write down every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment. Seeing the total is uncomfortable, but you cannot plan what you will not look at.

## Step 4: Choose your method

- **Avalanche**: pay minimums on everything and put all extra money on the highest-rate debt. Cheapest overall.
- **Snowball**: put extra money on the smallest balance first. You clear accounts sooner and stay motivated.

Run your own debts through the [Debt Payoff Planner](debt-payoff.html) to see the real difference. If it is small, choose the one you will stick with.

## Step 5: Lower your interest rates

- **Call your card issuers** and ask for a lower rate, especially if you have paid on time.
- **Balance transfer**: a 0% promotional card can save a lot if you clear the balance before the offer ends. Watch the transfer fee.
- **Consolidation loan**: a fixed-rate personal loan can replace several high-rate cards. Only do this if you will not run the cards up again.
- **Student loans**: check for income-based plans, refinancing options or forgiveness programs where you live.

## Step 6: Find extra money

Every extra dollar shortens the timeline. Cut a subscription, renegotiate a bill, sell unused items, take on overtime or a side job with a deadline. Send all windfalls — tax refunds, bonuses — straight to the target debt.

## Step 7: Stay motivated

- Put your debt-free date on your phone lock screen.
- Colour in a progress chart for each payment.
- Celebrate each paid-off account with something small and free.
- Tell one trusted person your goal.

## When to get help

If minimum payments take more than about a fifth of your take-home pay, you are being contacted by collectors, or you are borrowing to pay other debts, talk to a non-profit credit counsellor. They can explain options like debt management plans and help you avoid predatory "debt relief" companies.

## After debt

Redirect your old debt payments into your emergency fund and investments. You are used to living without that money — now it builds wealth instead of paying interest.
