Investing is how money grows faster than inflation. You do not need to pick stocks, watch the news or have a lot of money. You need a plan, low costs and time.

## Before you invest

Check three boxes first:

1. You have a starter **emergency fund**.
2. You are not carrying **high-interest debt** (credit cards, payday loans).
3. You know you will not need this money for at least **five years**.

## Step 1: Use tax-advantaged accounts first

Most countries offer retirement or tax-free savings accounts with valuable tax breaks. If your employer matches retirement contributions, contribute at least enough to get the full match — it is an instant return you will not find anywhere else.

## Step 2: Understand index funds

An **index fund** (or ETF) holds every company in a market index, such as a broad stock market. Instead of betting on a few companies, you own a small slice of hundreds or thousands. Index funds are popular because they are:

- **Diversified** — one company failing barely matters.
- **Cheap** — fees are often a fraction of actively managed funds.
- **Simple** — no stock picking required.

Research consistently finds that most actively managed funds fail to beat their index over long periods after fees.

## Step 3: Choose your mix

Your **asset allocation** — the split between stocks and bonds — matters more than any single fund. Stocks grow more but swing more; bonds are steadier.

- Long time horizon and calm nerves → more stocks.
- Need the money within 5–10 years or hate volatility → more bonds.

Target-date or "all-in-one" funds pick a mix for you and adjust it as you age — a good default for beginners.

## Step 4: Automate and ignore the noise

Set up an automatic monthly investment. Buying regularly through ups and downs (dollar-cost averaging) removes the temptation to time the market. Then check once or twice a year. Markets fall regularly; selling in a panic locks in losses.

## Step 5: Keep costs low

Fees compound too, in the wrong direction. Compare expense ratios and platform fees. See how much small differences matter with the [Compound Interest Calculator](compound-interest.html).

## Common beginner mistakes

- Waiting for the "right time" to start.
- Chasing last year's hot fund, stock or coin.
- Checking the portfolio daily.
- Investing money needed for next year's expenses.
- Paying high fees for advice you do not use.
