Sleep is the foundation every other upgrade stands on. Poor sleep makes it harder to focus, control appetite, manage mood and stick to any plan. Good sleep makes everything easier.

## How much do you need?

Most adults need **7 to 9 hours** a night. Teenagers need more. Very few people function well on less than seven hours over the long term, even if they feel used to it.

## Why it matters

During sleep your brain consolidates memories and clears waste, your body repairs tissue and regulates hormones that control hunger and stress. Short sleep is associated with weight gain, weaker immunity, higher blood pressure and poorer mental health.

## The seven most effective habits

1. **Fixed wake-up time**, seven days a week. This anchors your body clock more than a fixed bedtime.
2. **Morning daylight**: get outside within an hour of waking, even on cloudy days.
3. **Caffeine curfew**: caffeine can stay in your system for many hours; stop around 8 hours before bed.
4. **Cool, dark, quiet bedroom**: blackout curtains, earplugs or white noise if needed.
5. **Wind-down hour**: dim lights, put screens away or on night mode, and do something calm.
6. **Go easy on alcohol and late heavy meals**: alcohol may make you sleepy but fragments sleep later in the night.
7. **Bed is for sleep**: if you are awake for more than about 20 minutes, get up, do something relaxing in low light, and return when sleepy.

## Use sleep cycles

Sleep runs in roughly 90-minute cycles. Waking at the end of a cycle can feel easier. The [Sleep Cycle Calculator](sleep.html) suggests bedtimes for your wake-up time.

## Naps

A short nap of 10–20 minutes, early in the afternoon, can boost alertness. Longer or later naps may make night sleep harder.

## When to talk to a doctor

Speak with a doctor if you:

- Snore loudly, gasp or stop breathing during sleep
- Feel very sleepy during the day despite enough time in bed
- Have trouble sleeping most nights for three months or more
- Have restless or uncomfortable legs at night

Treatments such as cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) are highly effective and often work better long-term than sleeping pills.
