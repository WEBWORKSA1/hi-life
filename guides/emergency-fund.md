An emergency fund is the most boring money move you will ever make, and the one that changes your life the most. Without one, every flat tyre, vet bill or late paycheck lands on a credit card. With one, the same events become an inconvenience instead of a crisis. This guide shows you how to build it from zero, even if money is tight right now.

## Why it comes first

Most money plans fail for the same reason: something unexpected happens, there is no cash, and the plan gets paused while debt piles up. An emergency fund protects every other goal. It also changes how you feel. People with even a small cash buffer report less money stress and make better decisions at work and at home, because they are not one surprise away from trouble.

## Step 1: Know your number

Add up your **essential** monthly costs — the bills you would still pay if your income stopped tomorrow:

- Rent or mortgage, utilities and internet
- Basic groceries and household items
- Insurance premiums
- Transport to work
- Minimum payments on loans and cards

Leave out restaurants, subscriptions and shopping. The total is your monthly essentials figure. Your full target is usually **three to six months** of it, and more if your income is variable, you are the only earner, or people depend on you. ## Step 2: Set a starter goal you can hit fast

Six months of expenses can feel impossible. Do not start there. Your first target is **one month of essentials** (or a smaller round number if that is too far). Hitting a near goal quickly builds momentum, and it already covers most everyday emergencies.

## Step 3: Find the money

You rarely need a raise to start. Look for one-time cash and small recurring savings:

1. **Sell three things** you have not used in a year. Electronics, furniture and gear often add up fast.
2. **Cancel or pause** subscriptions you would not miss for a month.
3. **Redirect windfalls**: tax refunds, birthday money, bonuses, cashback.
4. **Run a "no-spend" fortnight** on wants and send the difference to savings.
5. **Pick up a short side gig** with a clear end date and goal.

## Step 4: Automate it

Open a separate savings account — ideally a high-yield one — and name it "Emergency Fund: Do Not Touch". Set an automatic transfer for the day after payday. Automation removes willpower from the equation; you save first and live on the rest. Even a small amount every payday builds a real buffer within a year.

## Step 5: Keep it safe and reachable

An emergency fund is insurance, not an investment. It should be:

- **Liquid**: reachable within a day or two.
- **Stable**: not in stocks or crypto that can fall right when you need them.
- **Separate**: not in your everyday account where it quietly gets spent.

A high-yield savings or money market account is ideal. Some people keep a small amount of cash at home as well, for short outages.

## What counts as an emergency?

Ask three questions: Is it **unexpected**? Is it **necessary**? Is it **urgent**? Job loss, urgent medical or dental bills, an essential car repair or emergency travel all qualify. A sale, a holiday or a planned purchase do not — give those their own "sinking fund" savings pots.

## After you use it

Using the fund is not failure; it is the point. Once the emergency is handled, pause extra spending and refill the fund before restarting other goals.

## Where it fits in your plan

A sensible order for most people:

1. Starter emergency fund (one month)
2. Capture any employer retirement match
3. Pay off high-interest debt
4. Build the full emergency fund
5. Invest for the long term
