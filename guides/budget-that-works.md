Most budgets fail because they are too detailed, too strict or too much work. A budget that works is simple enough that you keep using it. Here is a system you can set up in one evening and run in ten minutes a month.

## Start with what is real

Before you plan, look back. Download the last two or three months of bank and card statements and group spending into rough categories. Do not judge yet — just notice. Most people find two or three surprises: forgotten subscriptions, delivery apps, or "small" daily purchases that add up to a large monthly number.

## Use the 50/30/20 framework

Split your take-home pay into three buckets:

| Bucket | Share | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | ~50% | Housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments |
| Wants | ~30% | Dining out, shopping, travel, hobbies, streaming |
| Future | ~20% | Emergency fund, investing, retirement, extra debt payments |

Put your own numbers into the [50/30/20 Budget Calculator](budget.html) to see where you are over and by how much. It is a guide, not a law: in expensive cities needs may run higher, so protect the 20% by trimming wants.

## Create sinking funds for predictable "surprises"

Car repairs, gifts, annual insurance, school costs and holidays are not emergencies — they are predictable. Divide each yearly cost by 12 and save that amount monthly in a separate pot. When the bill arrives, the money is already there and your budget does not break.

## Automate the important things

Set up automatic transfers on payday:

1. Savings and investing (pay yourself first)
2. Sinking funds
3. Fixed bills

Whatever is left in your spending account is guilt-free money for groceries and fun. This is sometimes called a "reverse budget" and it works because good decisions happen by default.

## Cut the big three, not the small stuff

Skipping coffee helps a little. Fixing housing, transport and insurance helps a lot:

- **Housing**: negotiate rent at renewal, consider a roommate, or refinance if rates have dropped.
- **Transport**: a cheaper or paid-off car often saves more than every small cut combined.
- **Insurance**: shop your policies every year; loyalty rarely gets the best price.

## The 10-minute monthly money date

Once a month, sit down with your numbers:

1. Compare actual spending with your targets.
2. Move any leftover money to a goal.
3. Adjust next month's plan for known events.
4. Update your [net worth](net-worth.html).

Celebrate progress. A budget is a plan for your money, not a punishment.

## If you are a couple

Agree on shared goals first, then numbers. Many couples use a "yours, mine and ours" setup: a joint account for shared bills and goals, plus personal spending money each — no questions asked.

## Common mistakes

- Budgeting zero for fun, then giving up after a week.
- Forgetting irregular costs (use sinking funds).
- Tracking every cent forever. Track closely for a month, then use buckets.
