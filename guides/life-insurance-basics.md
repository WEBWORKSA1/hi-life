Life insurance is not for you. It is for the people who would struggle financially if you were gone. That makes it one of the most loving — and most avoided — money decisions you can make.

## Do you need it?

You probably need life insurance if:

- Someone depends on your income (partner, children, parents).
- You have a mortgage or debts someone else would inherit or co-signed.
- You are a stay-at-home parent whose work would be expensive to replace.
- You own a business with partners or key responsibilities.

You probably do not need it if you are single with no dependants and no co-signed debts, or already financially independent.

## How much cover?

Use the **DIME** method:

- **Debt**: non-mortgage debts plus final expenses.
- **Income**: your yearly income × the years your family would need it.
- **Mortgage**: the remaining balance.
- **Education**: expected costs for children.

Then subtract existing cover and savings your family could use. The [Life Insurance Needs Calculator](life-insurance.html) does this for you. A rough sanity check is 10–12 times your income.

## Term vs permanent

| | Term life | Permanent (whole/universal) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | Set period (10–30 years) | Lifetime |
| Cost | Much lower | Much higher |
| Cash value | None | Builds over time |
| Best for | Temporary needs: kids, mortgage, income | Estate planning, lifelong dependants |

For most families, term life covers the real need at the lowest cost. A common approach is to buy term and invest the difference.

## How to buy well

1. **Calculate your number first** so you are not sold a figure.
2. **Compare several quotes** directly or through an independent broker.
3. **Be honest on the application** — misstatements can void a claim.
4. **Lock in while young and healthy**; premiums rise with age and health changes.
5. **Ladder policies** (e.g. one 30-year and one 15-year) to match needs that shrink over time.
6. **Name beneficiaries** and review them after marriage, divorce or births.

## Work cover is not enough

Employer group cover is a nice bonus, but it is often only one or two times salary and usually ends when you leave the job.

## Other protection to consider

- **Disability / income protection**: you are statistically more likely to be unable to work for a period than to die during your career.
- **Critical illness cover** in some countries.
- **A will and guardianship plan** for children.
