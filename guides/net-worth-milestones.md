Net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. It is the scoreboard of personal finance — not because money is everything, but because it measures your freedom to choose.

## How to calculate it

**Assets**: cash, savings, investments, retirement accounts, the market value of your home and vehicles, and any business you own.

**Liabilities**: mortgage, car loans, student loans, credit cards, personal loans and money owed to others.

Net worth = assets − liabilities. The [Net Worth Calculator](net-worth.html) does the maths and lets you save private monthly snapshots.

## Milestones by decade (a rough guide)

Many planners use **multiples of salary** for retirement savings by age. One widely cited set of targets:

| Age | Retirement savings target |
|---|---|
| 30 | ~1× your salary |
| 40 | ~3× |
| 50 | ~6× |
| 60 | ~8× |
| 67 | ~10× |

Another classic yardstick says expected net worth ≈ age × gross income ÷ 10. These are signposts, not grades. Student loans, career changes, caring for family or living somewhere expensive can all shift your path.

## What actually grows net worth

1. **Spend less than you earn, every month.** The gap is the engine.
2. **Kill high-interest debt.** Paying off a 22% card is a guaranteed 22% return.
3. **Invest consistently** in diversified, low-cost funds and leave them alone.
4. **Grow your income.** Skills, promotions and job changes compound like interest.
5. **Avoid big depreciating purchases** on credit, especially cars.
6. **Protect it** with an emergency fund and the right insurance so one bad event does not reset you.

## Your twenties

Focus on habits and income. Build an emergency fund, capture any retirement match and invest small amounts automatically. Your biggest asset is your earning power.

## Your thirties and forties

Costs rise (homes, children) but so does income. Guard your savings rate when you get raises, review insurance and keep investing through market swings.

## Your fifties and beyond

Check your retirement plan yearly, consider extra catch-up contributions where allowed, reduce debt before retiring, and think about how you will turn savings into income.

## Do not compare to strangers

Averages are skewed by the very wealthy, and online posts are skewed by people who are doing well. The only fair comparison is you, a year ago. Track the trend, not the headline.
